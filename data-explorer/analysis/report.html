Voice gender classifier — report

Logistic regression on per-speaker median pitch (F0) and formants (F1/F2), extracted with Praat. 106 female + 105 male speakers across 2 datasets. (Binary only — no non-binary data yet.)

3. Is it really resonance, or just pitch in disguise?

We mined 34 speakers whose pitch is ≥1σ atypical for their gender (skewed toward the other gender). Two sobering results: (1) pitch-only is still 85% correct on them — male/female pitch barely overlaps in this read-speech data, so even atypical pitch rarely crosses the line; and (2) resonance-only is only 56% on these (near chance). Conclusion: our current resonance feature — median F1/F2 — is too weak to carry the hard cases. To actually separate resonance from pitch we need both (a) stronger resonance features (per-vowel normalization / vocal-tract-length estimation) and (b) data with genuine pitch overlap (Common Voice, trans voices).

We can't run this test cleanly yet — and that's itself a finding. Only 7 men fall in the 150–200 Hz overlap band (54 F / 7 M), far too imbalanced to isolate resonance from pitch. Our datasets barely overlap in pitch, which is also why pitch+resonance didn't beat pitch alone (there's almost no region where they disagree). Closing this gap needs more high-pitched men and low-pitched women — i.e. Common Voice and, ideally, trans voices.

4. Does it generalise across recording conditions?

6. A data-driven resonance score

The resonance-only model's probability (×100) is a calibrated 0–100 "brightness/femininity-of-resonance" score — a principled replacement for the hand-tuned bar in the web app.

Caveats. Small sample (~100 speakers); binary cis labels only (non-binary needs Common Voice); formants from a single fixed LPC ceiling; read speech only. Treat the numbers as directional, not final.
